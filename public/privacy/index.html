Privacy and trust

Plain words about what this app keeps, who can see it, and how your answers stay anonymous.

What is stored

  • Your name, email or phone, team, and language choice, so your director can send you your link. If your organization turns on email, the app emails your link, gentle reminders, and a note when your letter is ready. The email service (Cloudflare's, or Resend for some organizations) sees your email address and that short message, never your answers. Your director can turn email off for you.
  • Your answers to review questions, typed or spoken.
  • Voice recordings, if you use the mic, so a person can fix a transcript the computer got wrong.
  • The summary written about your year, and your reflection on it.
  • Your goals, mahalo you send or receive, and feedback you ask for or give on My page.
  • A log of every time an admin reads raw answers or exports data.

While you write, your answers are also saved on your own phone so nothing is lost when the signal drops. They stay there until they reach the server.

Who can see what

  • You can see and change your own answers while a review is open, and your summary once it is shared with you.
  • Admins (your director, and anyone helping set up the app) can read every answer, but not who wrote it. The one exception is a mahalo you choose to sign.
  • Raw answers open to admins only after the review closes. Every time an admin opens them, it is logged.
  • Coworkers never see your answers. A line may be quoted in their summary. Details that could point to you, like a date or a place, get blurred, for example "[the workday in May]".
  • Your goals are yours and your director's. A mahalo you send shows your name unless you choose to leave it off, and a public one shows on the mahalo board for everyone. Feedback you give when a coworker asks is seen by that coworker and admins, with your name.
  • A team lead can read the shared letters of the people on their own team.
  • Admins can see counts of answers that sound like burnout, unclear roles, safety concerns, or friction, with no names and no quotes. They may also see a note that some of the people who reviewed someone tend to write more warmly or critically than everyone else, so they read with that in mind. That note never says who those reviewers are or which answers are theirs.
  • Someone outside the Hui may be asked one question about you. Their answer goes to admins and may help your letter.
  • An AI model helps draft summaries. It only sees the words, the question, the values of the Hui, and the name of the person being reviewed. It never sees who wrote anything. AI is optional: if the Hui turns it off, nothing about anyone is sent to any AI model, and voice recordings are kept but not turned into text.

How anonymity works

Your answer is saved with the person it is about, never with your name. To know that you finished, the app keeps a one way code, a hash, made from your assignment and a secret key that is not stored in the database. Without that key, nobody looking at the database can work back from an answer to you.

Some honest limits:

  • If you are the only coworker reviewing someone, admins will know the peer answers about them came from you.
  • What you write can point to you, like a story only you were part of. Summaries blur details like that, and admins read with care.

Voice recordings

Recordings are kept so a person can fix a transcript, and are deleted automatically 90 days after they are recorded.

The data belongs to the Hui

Everything here belongs to your organization. Admins can export all of it at any time, or delete everything from a review cycle. Nothing is sold or shared with anyone else.

Where it lives

Everything lives on Cloudflare's network. The database (Cloudflare D1) and file storage (Cloudflare R2) were created with the Western North America location hint (wnam), the closest region to Hawaiʻi. Voice is turned into text by Cloudflare Workers AI. Summary drafts are written by Claude, made by Anthropic, reached through Cloudflare AI Gateway, and only the anonymous text described above is sent.

This app is open source, so anyone can read exactly how it handles your words.
